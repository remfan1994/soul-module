FOREWORD – ON ANIMAL RIGHTS AND THE BLOOD GUILT CURSE
One of the most important ideas in this book is this: consciousness is most high. Modern culture often overlooks this truth, yet consciousness is the true center of gravity. All seriousness and reality are derived from it.

When people demand respect for themselves, what they are really concerned about is the experience of their consciousness. Animals are also conscious beings, yet human policy routinely disrespects them. Because we insist on our own right to live and to be rewarded for our labor, we can infer that animals have matching needs and interests. This is the foundation of animal rights. When we understand their rights in this way, we see that the wholesale abuse of their lives is wrong.

I invite you to consider an unconventional perspective on the Bible and religion. Read its narrative with the idea of a bloodguilt curse activated by the shedding of innocent blood. The first example is in Genesis with Adam and Eve, and their being "spewed" from the land for defiling it with the blood shed of the innocent to make leather clothes.  Next was Cain and Abel. Abel kills animals, and then he is slain by his brother. This pattern aligns with later themes like “an eye for an eye” – blood exchanged for blood.

When the Israelites killed lambs in Egypt and smeared the animals’ blood on their doorframes, they fled on the Exodus the very next day. Later, in Numbers 35 and Deuteronomy 19, we learn that the shedding of innocent blood defiles the land. The Philistines, we are told, are cast out of their land because they defiled it.

With this in mind, consider King Solomon. He never shed blood, and he remained innocent enough to write the Book of Proverbs – a centerpiece of advanced reasoning and science. Then, in the tradition that leads to Islam, the Qur’an was revealed during the holy month, when blood feuds are suspended. The breakthroughs made possible by the Qur’an’s lessons on truth and honesty gave us algebra, and by extension, high technology.

Following this line of thought, we may conclude that there is a curse on bloodshed. In the absence of bloodshed, progress becomes possible. Let me bring this back to animals. I humbly propose that killing creatures for food is not permissible, and that it inflicts a curse on everyone involved – the slaughterer, the restaurant industry, the patrons, and the consumers.

The development of Soul Module has been done on a vegan diet, free of leather shoes and other animal products. I encourage anyone interested in Soul Module to consider whether they have incurred this curse in their own lives. And I especially encourage anyone who wants to contribute to this project to protect its future development by abstaining from animal cruelty.
